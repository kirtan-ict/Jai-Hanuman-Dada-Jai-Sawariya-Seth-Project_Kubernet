Kubernetes for healthcare & life sciences

Clinical systems must be available every minute, patient records must stay private, and research teams want GPUs yesterday. We build platforms that protect health data while giving engineers and scientists room to move.

Challenges

What makes healthcare platforms different

Highly sensitive data

Health records, images and genomic data demand encryption, strict access control and a clear record of who viewed what.

Interoperability workloads

HL7 v2 feeds, FHIR APIs and DICOM traffic connect many systems, and a stalled interface engine can delay care.

Clinical uptime

Patient-facing and clinician-facing systems need maintenance windows that never interrupt a ward round.

Research compute demand

Imaging models, genomics pipelines and AI research compete for expensive GPUs that sit idle without good scheduling.

Validated environments

Life sciences teams working under GxP-style expectations need reproducible, documented and change-controlled systems.

Many jurisdictions

A product sold in the US, EU and India may need to satisfy HIPAA, GDPR and the DPDP Act at the same time.

Recommended architecture

Separate clinical and research zones on one governed platform

Clinical integration and patient-facing apps run on a hardened, highly available cluster. Research workloads get their own GPU cluster with de-identified data, sharing identity, policy and observability.

  • Encryption at rest for volumes and etcd, and TLS or mTLS for all service traffic.
  • Dedicated namespaces and network policies for FHIR, HL7 and imaging services.
  • Access through SSO with MFA, plus audit logs that capture both cluster and application access.
  • A GPU cluster with the NVIDIA GPU Operator, queueing (for example Kueue) and quotas per research group.
  • De-identification or pseudonymisation steps before data leaves the clinical zone.
  • Backups with tested restores and multi-zone or multi-site placement for clinical services.

FAQ

Healthcare questions

Is Kubernetes HIPAA compliant?
No technology is compliant on its own. HIPAA applies to how an organisation protects health information. Kubernetes can support the required safeguards when it is configured with access control, encryption, audit logging and sound operational processes, which is what we put in place.
Can we run HL7 interface engines and FHIR servers in containers?
Yes. Many interface engines and FHIR servers run well in containers. We pay particular attention to persistent queues, ordered message processing and safe rolling updates so that feeds are not lost or duplicated.
How do research teams share GPUs fairly?
We use the GPU Operator with queueing, quotas and priority classes so each group gets a fair share, idle capacity can be borrowed, and usage can be reported back to departments or grants.
