Kubernetes for retail & e-commerce

Your busiest hour of the year is also the worst time for an outage. We build platforms that scale up for sale days and festive seasons, scale back down afterwards, and keep stores trading even when the network does not cooperate.

Challenges

What makes retail platforms different

Extreme, predictable peaks

Flash sales, festive seasons and campaign launches can multiply traffic within minutes, then drop away just as fast.

Latency costs conversions

Shoppers in different cities and countries expect fast search, product pages and checkout wherever they are.

Hundreds of small sites

Stores and warehouses run POS, inventory and camera workloads on small hardware with no IT staff on site.

Card data in the mix

Checkout and in-store payments bring PCI DSS obligations that should not spread to the whole catalogue platform.

Thin margins

Capacity bought for the peak but kept all year erodes margin. Cost per order matters as much as uptime.

Fast release cycles

Merchandising and growth teams ship often, so releases must be safe, reversible and frozen cleanly during peak events.

Recommended architecture

Elastic cloud core with a fleet of store-edge clusters

Customer-facing services run on managed Kubernetes in one or more regions behind a CDN. Stores and warehouses run lightweight clusters managed centrally through GitOps.

  • Horizontal pod autoscaling on request rate or queue depth, plus Karpenter or Cluster Autoscaler for nodes.
  • Pre-scaling schedules and load tests ahead of known sale events.
  • A CDN and regional clusters or read replicas to keep latency low for distant shoppers.
  • A separate, tightly scoped payment zone to limit PCI DSS scope.
  • K3s or a similar lightweight distribution in stores, with local caching so tills keep working offline.
  • Fleet-wide GitOps so one change can roll out to every store in controlled waves.
Compliance & security controls

From requirement to Kubernetes control

RequirementHow we implement it on Kubernetes
PCI DSS: protect cardholder data and limit the systems in scopeCheckout and payment services on dedicated nodes or clusters, default-deny network policies, tokenisation at the edge of the zone, and no card data in logs.
PCI DSS: secure configuration, patching and vulnerability managementCIS-benchmarked node images, regular scanning of images and nodes, and automated upgrade pipelines tested before peak freezes.
Logging and monitoring of access to payment systemsAPI audit logs, ingress logs and runtime detection (for example Falco) forwarded to a central SIEM.
Customer data privacy (for example India's DPDP Act or the EU GDPR)Region pinning for personal data, field-level masking in logs and traces, and documented data retention for each service.
Security of remote store devicesDisk encryption, signed images, remote attestation where hardware allows, and certificate-based enrolment of each store cluster.
Availability during peak tradingPod disruption budgets, multi-zone node pools, load-tested capacity plans and a change freeze enforced in GitOps.
Not legal advice. PCI DSS and privacy laws are summarised in general terms. Your QSA, compliance team and counsel should confirm your scope and obligations.

FAQ

Retail questions

Can autoscaling alone handle a flash sale?
Usually not on its own. New nodes take time to arrive, so we combine reactive autoscaling with scheduled pre-scaling, warm capacity buffers and load tests based on your expected peak.
What happens to a store cluster if the internet goes down?
Store workloads are designed to run locally, with local data caches and queued sync. When connectivity returns, data and configuration reconcile automatically through GitOps.
How do we keep the whole platform out of PCI DSS scope?
By isolating payment services on their own nodes or clusters, restricting network paths into that zone and using tokenisation so other services never handle card numbers. Your QSA makes the final scope decision.
Can we use spot or preemptible instances?
Yes, for stateless and batch workloads with proper disruption handling. We keep checkout and other critical paths on on-demand capacity.
