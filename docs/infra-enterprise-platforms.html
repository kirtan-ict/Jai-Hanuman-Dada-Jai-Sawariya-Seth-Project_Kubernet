Enterprise Kubernetes platforms

OpenShift, Rancher, Tanzu and Mirantis, without the sales pitch

Commercial platforms bundle Kubernetes with an operating system, tooling and a support contract. They can save a lot of assembly work. We are not a reseller, so we help you pick the one that suits you, implement it well, and move between them if you need to.

  • Red Hat OpenShift
  • ROSA
  • ARO
  • SUSE Rancher
  • RKE2
  • VMware Tanzu
  • Mirantis Kubernetes Engine

When this fits

  • Auditors or internal policy require a vendor-supported, certified distribution.
  • You want one integrated stack (OS, registry, CI, monitoring, console) rather than assembling it.
  • Many teams of mixed experience need guardrails and a self-service console.
  • You already have a strategic relationship with Red Hat, SUSE, Broadcom / VMware or Mirantis.

When it doesn't

  • You run entirely in one public cloud and the provider's managed service already covers your needs.
  • Subscription cost per core outweighs the operational effort it saves at your scale.
  • Your team prefers upstream tooling and is comfortable assembling the stack.
  • You need features faster than the vendor's release cadence delivers them.

Reference architecture

What you buy, and what you still own

A platform subscription covers the distribution, its operating system and the bundled tooling. It does not decide your tenancy model, network design, identity integration, backup strategy or how applications are onboarded. That is where most projects succeed or stall.

  • Tenancy model: projects or namespaces per team, quotas and network isolation.
  • Integration with corporate identity, certificates, proxies, SIEM and CMDB.
  • Hardening aligned with CIS benchmarks and your internal security baseline.
  • Golden paths so teams deploy the approved way by default.
Implementation

What we implement

  • Vendor-neutral platform selection with a weighted scorecard and proof of concept.
  • Installation: OpenShift IPI / UPI or Agent-based installs, ROSA and ARO, Rancher on RKE2, Tanzu on vSphere, MKE.
  • Identity, RBAC and multi-tenancy design across teams and environments.
  • Operator lifecycle and add-on management (OperatorHub, Rancher apps, Helm).
  • CI/CD and GitOps (OpenShift GitOps / Pipelines, Fleet, Argo CD, Tekton).
  • Backup with OADP or Velero, and DR between sites.
  • Migration between platforms: workload inventory, API translation (for example Routes to Ingress or Gateway API, SCCs to Pod Security Standards), data moves and cut-over.

Lessons learned

Common pitfalls we prevent

  • Choosing on a demo rather than a proof of concept with your own workloads.
  • Leaning on proprietary APIs everywhere, which makes leaving expensive later.
  • Skipping minor versions until the upgrade path becomes a multi-step project.
  • Under-counting cores for licensing and getting a large true-up bill.
  • Leaving defaults untouched: open self-provisioning, no quotas, no network policies.
  • Assuming the vendor handles everything; support contracts cover the product, not your configuration.

FAQ

Enterprise platform questions

Related services: platform assessment, platform engineering, migration, training, and support.

Are you partnered with any of these vendors?
Our recommendations are not tied to resale margins. We will tell you when a managed cloud service or upstream Kubernetes is the better answer, even if that means no platform subscription at all.
Is OpenShift "just Kubernetes"?
It is a certified Kubernetes distribution with a lot added on top: an immutable OS, operators for nearly every component, stricter security defaults and its own developer tooling. Standard Kubernetes manifests usually work, but images that run as root or rely on privileged access may need changes.
Can Rancher manage our OpenShift or cloud clusters?
Rancher can import and manage most conformant clusters, including EKS, AKS and GKE. Depth of management varies by cluster type, so we check the specific features you need.
How hard is migrating from one platform to another?
It depends mostly on how much platform-specific functionality your apps use. Plain Deployments, Services and Helm charts move easily; proprietary build systems, routes, security constraints and operators take more work. We start with an inventory that quantifies this.
