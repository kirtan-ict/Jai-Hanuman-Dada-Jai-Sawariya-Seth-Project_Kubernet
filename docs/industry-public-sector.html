Kubernetes for government & public sector

Citizen services must be secure, available and accessible to everyone, often on infrastructure the department controls end to end. We build sovereign Kubernetes platforms, including fully air-gapped ones, with long support lifecycles and documentation that survives staff changes.

Challenges

What makes public-sector platforms different

Data residency and sovereignty

Citizen data, logs and backups must stay within the country and under clear government control.

Air-gapped networks

Sensitive systems may have no internet access at all, which changes how images, updates and patches arrive.

Supply-chain trust

Every component must be traceable and verified before it enters a government network.

Services for everyone

Citizen portals must work for people using screen readers, slow connections and low-cost phones.

Sudden demand spikes

Result announcements, scheme deadlines and filing dates create short, intense surges in traffic.

Procurement and audit

Projects are tendered, audited and handed over between vendors, so documentation and open standards matter.

Recommended architecture

A sovereign platform with an air-gapped option

Public-facing services run in a government data centre or on a MeitY-empanelled cloud offering in India. Sensitive internal systems run in a separate air-gapped enclave that receives software only through a controlled transfer process.

  • Clusters in government data centres or empanelled cloud regions inside India, including backups and logs.
  • A private registry and package mirror; images are scanned, signed and transferred into the enclave through a one-way or controlled process.
  • Admission policies that accept only signed images from internal registries.
  • Central audit logging and integration with the department's security operations and incident reporting process.
  • Autoscaling and load testing for announcement days on the public-facing tier.
  • Open-source components and full runbooks, so the platform is not locked to any single vendor.

FAQ

Public-sector questions

Can Kubernetes run with no internet connection at all?
Yes. Air-gapped clusters are installed from offline bundles, pull images from an internal registry and receive updates through a controlled transfer process. Monitoring, logging and GitOps all run inside the enclave.
Do we have to use a MeitY-empanelled cloud?
That depends on your department's policies and the nature of the workload. We can design for empanelled cloud offerings, your own data centres or a mix, and keep the platform portable between them.
How does the DPDP Act affect our platform?
The Act sets obligations for how personal data is collected, used, protected and erased. At the infrastructure level that means sound security safeguards, clear data retention and deletion, and the ability to investigate and report breaches. Your legal team should interpret the specific duties for your department.
Will we be locked in to KubeSetu?
No. We build on open-source components and open standards, keep everything in your repositories and hand over runbooks and training so your teams or another partner can operate the platform.
