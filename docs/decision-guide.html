Operating models

Managed vs self-managed vs enterprise distribution

Whichever infrastructure you land on, you also choose who runs the control plane and who you call when it breaks.

AspectManaged serviceSelf-managed (upstream / open source)Enterprise distribution
ExamplesEKS, AKS, GKE, OKE, DOKS, LKEkubeadm, RKE2, k3s, k0s, TalosOpenShift, Rancher Prime, Tanzu, Mirantis Kubernetes Engine
Control plane run byCloud providerYour team (or a partner)Your team, with vendor tooling and support; some offered as managed services
UpgradesProvider-assisted; you schedule and handle nodes and add-onsFully yours, including etcd and certificatesVendor-tested upgrade paths; you run them
FlexibilityLimited to the provider's optionsHighest; any component, any versionModerate; within the vendor's supported stack
Where it runsThat provider's cloud (some offer on-premises variants)AnywhereAnywhere the vendor supports
SupportProvider support plan for the serviceCommunity, plus a partner if you chooseVendor subscription covering the whole stack
Cost modelPer-cluster fee plus compute; opexNo licence; hardware and people timeSubscription (often per core) plus infrastructure
Skills neededModerateHighModerate to high
Best forCloud-first teams that want to focus on applicationsExperienced teams, edge, bare metal, cost-sensitive scaleRegulated enterprises wanting one supported platform
Learn moreManaged cloudOn-premises, bare metal, edgeEnterprise platforms
