Air-gapped, sovereign and regulated

Kubernetes with no route to the internet

Some environments cannot pull an image, a Helm chart or an OS update from the internet, ever. We design the supply chain that brings software across the gap in a controlled, verifiable way, and the clusters that run happily on the other side.

  • Harbor
  • Image mirroring
  • Offline installers
  • Internal PKI
  • SBOM
  • cosign

When this fits

  • Government, defence and critical-infrastructure systems with classified or isolated networks.
  • Banking, financial services and insurance environments where regulators expect strict egress control.
  • Data sovereignty rules require software and data to stay within a national boundary or operator.
  • Industrial and OT networks that must stay segmented from IT and the internet.

When it doesn't

  • A restricted-egress design (private endpoints, proxy allow-lists) would meet the requirement at much lower effort.
  • Teams expect to pull new open-source tools on demand; every new component here needs an import process.
  • Nobody can own the import pipeline, vulnerability review and offline update cadence.

Not sure which level of isolation you need? Our decision guide asks the right questions.

Reference architecture

A one-way, verifiable supply chain

The security of an air-gapped cluster rests on what you let across the gap. We build an import pipeline on the connected side that produces a signed, scanned and documented bundle, and a matching set of services on the isolated side that serve it and refuse anything else.

  • Approved inputs only: images, charts and packages pinned to digests from an allow-list.
  • Evidence attached: vulnerability reports, SBOMs and signatures travel with each artifact.
  • Enforcement inside: admission policy verifies signatures against your keys.
  • Own trust anchors: an internal CA for every certificate, with no public CA dependency.
Implementation

What we implement

  • Offline installation of the distribution using vendor air-gap bundles (RKE2, OpenShift disconnected installs, Talos, kubeadm with pre-staged images).
  • Harbor (or your existing registry) with projects, replication, vulnerability scanning and retention rules.
  • Image and chart mirroring tooling such as Hauler, oc-mirror, Zarf or skopeo, with digest-pinned manifests.
  • Internal PKI: an offline root, issuing CAs, and cert-manager for automated certificate rotation inside the clusters.
  • Supply-chain controls: SBOM generation, cosign signing and attestation, and admission-time verification.
  • Offline OS and Kubernetes update bundles with a documented, repeatable import procedure.
  • Hardening against CIS benchmarks and, where required, DISA STIGs, with evidence for auditors.
  • Internal-only observability, audit logging and SIEM integration.

Day-2 operations

Day-2 support we provide

With no internet, every update is a planned import. Our support plans can include on-site work, or fully documented procedures for your own staff, where your rules require it.

Upgrades

Quarterly or agreed-cadence upgrade bundles, built and tested on a connected staging copy, then imported and rolled out with the same steps every time.

Patching

Out-of-cycle security bundles when a critical CVE lands, with impact analysis using the SBOMs already on file.

Monitoring

Fully internal monitoring and alerting, certificate expiry tracking for the internal CA, and registry storage and scan health.

Backup and DR

etcd and volume backups to internal object storage, offline copies where policy demands, and restore tests that do not depend on outside services.

Scaling

Adding nodes and clusters from offline images, and growing the registry and mirrors as the catalogue of approved software expands.

Incident response

Diagnosis with only the tools already inside the boundary, plus a pre-approved emergency import path for fixes.

Lessons learned

Common pitfalls we prevent

  • Hidden internet dependencies: Helm charts, operators or init containers that fetch from public URLs at runtime.
  • Images referenced by tag instead of digest, so what runs is not what was reviewed.
  • Internal CA expiry with no rotation plan, taking down every service at once.
  • Upgrade bundles built by hand and impossible to reproduce for auditors.
  • Falling many versions behind because each import was treated as a one-off project.
  • Unsigned "temporary" images that bypass policy and never get removed.

FAQ

Air-gapped questions

Related services: security and compliance, cluster build, platform engineering, and support.

Which distributions work well offline?
Most major distributions provide documented air-gapped installs. RKE2 and OpenShift have mature offline tooling; Talos and kubeadm work well with pre-staged images. The choice usually depends on your support and certification requirements.
How do we keep up with security patches?
By making imports routine rather than exceptional: a regular cadence for planned updates and a pre-approved fast path for critical fixes. SBOMs let you quickly tell which clusters are affected by a new vulnerability.
Do you support specific compliance frameworks?
We map controls to the frameworks your organisation follows, such as CIS benchmarks, DISA STIGs, ISO 27001, or national and sector regulators' guidelines, and produce evidence for your auditors. Formal certification is granted by your assessors, not by us.
Can your engineers work on-site?
On-site work can be arranged where your rules require it; any personnel clearance requirements are agreed during scoping. We can also hand over fully documented procedures so your own cleared staff perform every step inside the boundary.
