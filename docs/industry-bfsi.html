Kubernetes for banking, financial services & insurance

Modern digital channels need the speed of containers. Regulators, auditors and risk committees need proof that every change, access and byte of customer data is controlled. We build platforms that satisfy both.

Challenges

What makes BFSI platforms different

Data must stay in-country

Payment and customer data often has to be stored in India, which shapes where clusters, backups and log pipelines can run.

Everything must be auditable

Inspectors expect to see who changed what, when and with whose approval, across clusters that change many times a day.

Cardholder data scope

Without careful segmentation, one shared cluster can pull every workload into PCI DSS scope and multiply audit effort.

Proven disaster recovery

Recovery objectives must be defined, documented and demonstrated in drills, not just assumed from a backup schedule.

Constant attack pressure

Financial services are a prime target. Images, APIs and identities all need hardening, monitoring and fast patching.

Legacy cores alongside new apps

Core banking and policy admin systems rarely move first. Containers must integrate with them securely and predictably.

Recommended architecture

A segmented, in-country platform with a warm DR site

We usually run production on in-country data centres or a private cloud, with digital channels optionally on an in-region managed cloud. The pattern below keeps regulated workloads isolated and every action traceable.

  • Separate clusters or dedicated node pools for the cardholder data environment, with default-deny network policies.
  • Ingress through a web application firewall and API gateway, with mutual TLS between services.
  • Secrets held in Vault or an HSM-backed key service, never in plain manifests.
  • Admission policies that block unsigned images, privileged pods and unapproved registries.
  • Kubernetes API audit logs and GitOps history shipped to your SIEM with retention you control.
  • A second site in-country with replicated state, Velero backups and scheduled failover drills.

Tamper-evident audit trailsKubernetes API audit policy, GitOps commit and approval history, and immutable forwarding to your SIEM with agreed retention.
Business continuity and disaster recoveryDefined RPO and RTO per service, cross-site replication, Velero backups with restore tests, and scheduled DR drills with written reports.
Secure software supply chainImage scanning in CI, signed images verified at admission, private registry mirrors, and an SBOM for every release.

FAQ

BFSI questions

Can Kubernetes be used for PCI DSS workloads?
Yes. PCI DSS does not prohibit containers. What matters is that the cardholder data environment is clearly segmented, access is controlled and logged, and systems are hardened and patched. We design cluster and network boundaries to keep that scope as small as practical.
Do we have to stay on-premises to meet localisation rules?
Not necessarily. Many organisations use cloud regions located in India for some workloads. The key is to ensure that data, backups, logs and replicas all remain within approved locations, and that your outsourcing and risk processes cover the provider.
How do you produce evidence for auditors?
Because the platform is defined in Git, we can show approved changes, policy definitions and their enforcement history. Cluster audit logs and policy reports are exported to your SIEM, and we prepare a control-to-evidence mapping during the project.
Will you work with our existing OpenShift or Tanzu licences?
Yes. We build on upstream Kubernetes and on enterprise distributions, and can harden and operate what you already own rather than replacing it.
